    How harmonization works

    NASA flies two kinds of fire sensors: MODIS (Terra, Aqua; 1 km pixels) and VIIRS (Suomi NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21; 375 m pixels). Counting raw detections would double count the same fire. A detection means a sensor saw heat consistent with fire in that pixel; it does not mean the whole cell burned. So every detection is snapped to one common ~1 km grid, the coarser MODIS footprint. A cell-day counts once, no matter how many sensors or overpasses saw it. Because a MODIS pixel can sit a cell away from the VIIRS pixels of the same fire, a cell counts as seen by both when the other sensor fired in that cell or one next to it on the same day. Confidence is mapped to one low / nominal / high scale (MODIS: below 30, 30–79, 80 and up). Fire radiative power takes the strongest single overpass per sensor, then the larger of the two sensors, so nothing is added twice. Days are local solar days.

    Sources used
